Us Holding Hands


The first time we held hands, neither of us planned it.

It was raining that afternoon, the kind of rain that turned the streets silver and made everyone rush for shelter. We were standing beneath the narrow roof of a little café, watching the world disappear behind curtains of water.

“You’re going to get soaked,” I said.

She smiled. “So are you.”

“I’m not going anywhere.”

“Neither am I.”

For a moment, we simply stood there, shoulder to shoulder. I could hear the soft tapping of rain against the roof and the quiet rhythm of her breathing. Then, almost without thinking, her fingers brushed against mine.

I froze.

She looked at me, and I looked back at her.

Neither of us said anything.

Slowly, carefully, I moved my hand closer. Our fingers touched again, and this time, she didn't pull away. Instead, she slipped her hand into mine.

It was such a simple thing.

But somehow, it felt like the entire world had become quiet just for us.

Her hand was warm. Mine was slightly cold from the rain. She squeezed my fingers gently, and I smiled.

“Why are you smiling?” she asked.

“Because I like this.”

“This?”

“Us.”

She looked down at our hands, then back at me.

“I like us too.”

After that day, holding hands became our little language.

We held hands while walking through crowded streets, as if we were afraid the world might separate us. We held hands at the cinema when the lights went off. We held hands when sitting beside the ocean, watching the sun sink beneath the horizon. Sometimes we didn't even talk. We simply sat together, our fingers intertwined, comfortable in the silence.

Years passed.

Life wasn't always kind to us. We argued. We misunderstood each other. There were nights when one of us cried and mornings when neither wanted to speak.

But somehow, we always found our way back.

One evening, after a particularly difficult day, we sat together on a bench beneath the same kind of gray sky as the day we first met. She looked tired.

“Do you ever wonder where we'll be ten years from now?” she asked.

I looked at her hand resting beside mine.

“Hopefully, right here.”

She laughed softly. “That's your big plan?”

I took her hand.

“No,” I said. “My plan is simpler than that.”

“What is it?”

“To keep holding your hand.”

She rested her head against my shoulder.

The rain began to fall again, gently at first, then harder.

Neither of us moved.

We had no idea what the future would bring. We didn't know where life would take us or what challenges waited ahead.

But as long as her hand was in mine, I knew I didn't have to face any of it alone.

And years later, whenever someone asked me when I knew she was the one, I never mentioned the first kiss or the first “I love you.”

I always remembered that rainy afternoon.

Two hands finding each other.

Two hearts choosing to stay.

Us, holding hands.
